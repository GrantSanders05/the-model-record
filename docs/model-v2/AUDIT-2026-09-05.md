# The Model — Deep Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-05  
**Repository:** `GrantSanders05/the-model-record`  
**Scope:** CFB prediction engine, grades, market data, backtests, calibration, ledger/tracking, best-bets layer, automation, persistence, roster/availability tooling, public research output, and recent model-development process.

## Executive verdict

The Model is **not junk**. In fact, its auditability and willingness to expose failures are strong compared with most personal betting models. It already has chronological backtesting, an append-only pick concept, line history, validation harnesses, model calibration tools, health checks, and explicit warnings when an edge is not proven.

The biggest risk is no longer basic coding quality. The biggest risk is **research validity**:

1. the same small body of hand-grade data has been examined repeatedly while model rules were changed;
2. the published record mixes several concepts that should be distinct;
3. market data are not yet captured with enough price/provider/timestamp detail to reconstruct exactly what was bettable;
4. the live model and the “Best Bets” strategy do not yet have one canonical definition of what counts as an official signal;
5. persistent state is being treated partly as an Actions cache even though some of that state cannot be regenerated;
6. several of the most valuable new inputs are available but are not wired into the production grade model.

**Recommendation:** preserve the current model as the Champion, fix measurement/provenance/persistence first, then build challengers. Do not make 2026 an endless tuning loop.

# 1. What is already strong

## 1.1 Chronological prediction-before-observation

`backtest.py` walks games in chronological order, predicts before the result is observed, and only then updates the model. That is the correct basic shape for a walk-forward sports backtest and is materially safer than calculating season-end ratings and replaying them backward.

## 1.2 The project admits when public-data models fail

The README records that the tested results-only/public-data models did not beat the spread out of sample and explicitly labels the hand-grade result “encouraging, not proven.” Keep that culture. A model that records failed experiments is much easier to improve honestly than one that deletes them.

## 1.3 The ledger concept is correct

The append-only philosophy is exactly right. A prediction should be written before kickoff and should never be edited to match later knowledge. The repository has also already discovered and repaired serious stale-lock/missed-lock problems rather than hiding them.

## 1.4 The code has multiple anti-self-deception safeguards

Useful existing pieces include:

- `validate_harness.py` and control/oracle concepts;
- explicit train/test separation in `optimize.py`;
- preference for RMSE/MAE over directly optimizing ATS percentage;
- confidence intervals around ATS results;
- blowout and unrated-game exclusions on the Best Bets board;
- calibration checks;
- a line-history table;
- stale-lock and missed-lock diagnostics;
- warnings that large model disagreement has historically not implied equally large realized edge.

These should be expanded, not replaced.

## 1.5 Your hand grades are potentially the most valuable asset

The unique part of this project is not Elo, PPA, rankings, or the sportsbook line. Everyone can access versions of those. The scarce input is the human football evaluation. V2 should make those grades *more useful* by turning them into matchup-specific features and evaluating whether they predict market movement and/or game residuals.

# 2. P0 findings — fix before trusting a headline record

## P0-A — The published ATS grade and the locked wager are not the same object

### Current behavior

The ledger stores `market_margin_at_pick`, later fills `closing_margin`, and grades ATS primarily against the **closing margin**. That answers:

> “Was the model on the side that beat the final market?”

It does **not** necessarily answer:

> “Did the published pick win at the line we locked?”

If a side was locked +3.5 and closed +2.5, a final margin of -3 wins the published wager but loses against the closing line. Those are two different facts.

### Required change

Track both forever:

- `ats_result_at_pick`
- `ats_result_at_close`
- `spread_line_at_pick`
- `spread_price_at_pick`
- `closing_spread_line`
- `closing_spread_price`

For totals, do the same with line and price.

### Reporting rule

The strategy's official W-L and ROI must use the **locked line/price**. Closing-line performance gets its own diagnostic card.

Historical rows can have at-pick ATS re-derived because the at-pick line and final score exist. Historical spread ROI cannot be fully reconstructed unless the actual spread juice was stored; if it was not, label any -110 ROI as synthetic.

---

## P0-B — “All forecasts” and “Best Bets” are different strategies

`predict.generate()` creates a side for essentially every priced non-tie disagreement. `best_bets.rank()` then excludes games such as unrated matchups and lines outside the structural range of the model.

The ledger, however, is fed from generated picks rather than a canonical strategy-signal object. That means the public prediction record can contain games the Best Bets layer says should not be played.

### Required schema separation

Create:

1. **`forecast_log`** — every model forecast, even no-bets;
2. **`signal_log`** — only decisions that passed the strategy rules at that moment;
3. **`bet_log`** — actual wagers, if any.

A game can have a forecast and no signal. That is normal.

### Why this matters

Without this separation, “the model went X-Y” can mean either:

- every forecast,
- every lined game,
- every game inside the ±28 range,
- every fully graded game,
- or the actual Best Bets strategy.

Those should never share one headline number.

---

## P0-C — 2025 is development data now, not a clean final test set

The 2025 grade season has been used to:

- choose and remove terms;
- fit scale;
- split grade and quality coefficients;
- fit edge-realization shrinkage;
- inspect early/late behavior;
- compare quality-point formulas;
- examine edge buckets;
- identify blowout handling;
- diagnose moneyline EV failure;
- make multiple production decisions.

Even when each individual script has a train/test split, the *human developer* has repeatedly seen the “test” results and then changed the system. That turns the test set into a de facto validation set.

### Required rule

**2026 becomes prospective evidence.**

Freeze the current Champion. Every new model produces shadow forecasts before games. Do not tune a challenger using the same 2026 outcomes you later quote as its validation.

If a real bug is fixed midseason, create a new version and keep the old predictions in their original version bucket.

---

## P0-D — GitHub Actions cache is not a safe source of truth for unique state

The workflow currently restores/saves the SQLite database and caches. GitHub's own documentation says caches are intended for reusable files such as dependencies/intermediate outputs and that jobs should be able to recover if a cache is unavailable. GitHub can evict caches after inactivity and under storage pressure.

That is acceptable for CFBD response caches. It is not acceptable as the only durable home for:

- pick history;
- line-history observations;
- model versions attached to picks;
- unique grade snapshots;
- experiment results that cannot be reproduced from public APIs.

### Required change

Make SQLite a **materialized working database**, not the canonical event store.

Persist unique events durably in a source-controlled/state store:

- market quote events;
- grade snapshots;
- model forecasts;
- official signals;
- final results;
- void events;
- model/config registry.

A practical zero-new-service approach is an append-only JSONL event journal on a dedicated `data-state` branch, with SQLite rebuildable from those files. If a database service is later used, still export immutable daily snapshots.

---

## P0-E — A model forecast is not reproducible from `config_label` alone

A label such as `cfb_grades@2026-09-05` is not enough when code, defaults, grades, aliases, provider selection, or a config can change on the same day.

Each forecast should persist:

- `model_id`
- `model_version`
- Git commit SHA
- config SHA-256
- feature-schema version
- grade-snapshot ID/hash for both teams
- market-quote ID
- injury/availability snapshot ID
- weather snapshot ID if used
- generated timestamp
- target decision horizon (e.g. T-72h, T-24h, T-2h)
- code/data source versions where practical

Then a forecast can be reproduced months later.

---

## P0-F — The research bundle has a privacy footgun

`research_export.py` still builds and exports a `mybets` object sourced from the legacy sheet path. The browser-entered bets are currently local-only, but legacy sheet bets can enter a JSON bundle that is publicly served by the research site.

### Required change

Default public export to a strict allow-list. Do not serialize any personal wager fields unless an explicit `--include-private-bets` mode is used for a non-public build.

Automated test:

- fail the public build if keys such as `mybets`, `stake`, `book`, personal ROI, or account-specific values appear in the published bundle;
- alternatively keep `mybets` as a status-only object with no wager rows.

# 3. P1 measurement problems

## P1-A — `metrics.py` uses the label `clv_mean` for something that is not CLV

The metric is the mean absolute difference between model margin and market margin. That is **model disagreement**, not closing-line value.

There is already a more legitimate directional CLV calculation in tracking. Consolidate these definitions into one module and rename the current metric, e.g.:

- `mean_abs_market_disagreement`

True CLV requires a market observation at the wager timestamp and a later closing observation.

## P1-B — Raw spread points are an incomplete CLV metric

A half-point is not equally valuable everywhere in football. Moving from +2.5 to +3 is economically different from moving +15.5 to +16. Juice changes also matter.

Track at least two versions:

1. **Line CLV:** directional points gained/lost.
2. **Price CLV:** change in implied/fair probability at a comparable line or closing market value.

When exact alternate-line curves are unavailable, keep price CLV and point CLV separate rather than pretending one converts perfectly into the other.

## P1-C — Spread/total prices are missing from the model ledger

Moneylines carry actual odds, but spread and total backtests largely assume -110. Going forward, every quote should include:

- provider/book;
- spread line;
- home spread price;
- away spread price;
- total;
- over price;
- under price;
- moneylines;
- observation time.

Without price, a +3 -125 and +3 -105 look identical to the model even though the economics are materially different.

## P1-D — Current market ingestion collapses providers too early

The database defines a provider priority and retains one preferred line per game. That is convenient for display, but it throws away information useful for research:

- consensus median;
- best available price;
- sharp-vs-soft disagreement;
- market dispersion;
- provider-specific movement;
- whether a model is only beating a stale book.

V2 should ingest **all provider quotes into `market_quotes`** and derive display/decision views afterward.

## P1-E — Exact decision horizons are not standardized

The old three-day locking window created a major problem: a Saturday game could receive an “official” pick days before kickoff, then remain frozen while grades, injuries, and lines changed.

Record forecasts at standardized horizons:

- T-72h
- T-24h
- T-6h
- T-2h
- optional T-30m
- close

Choose one horizon as the official strategy lock (recommended starting point: T-2h, with a small grace window). Earlier horizons remain valuable for measuring information arrival and line-movement skill.

## P1-F — “Close” needs a formal definition

Document exactly what closing line means:

- last consensus quote before kickoff?
- final DraftKings quote?
- CFBD's final stored line?
- median of selected books within X minutes before kickoff?

Do not mix these across games. Store the rule/version as metadata.

# 4. P1 model-design problems

## P1-G — The current grade model mostly reduces rich film grades to one scalar difference

Position groups are valuable specifically because matchups matter. A total rating leaves much of that information unused.

Build matchup residual features such as:

- home OL vs away DL;
- away OL vs home DL;
- home QB + WR vs away DB;
- away QB + WR vs home DB;
- home RB + OL vs away DL + LB;
- away RB + OL vs home DL + LB;
- special-teams differential;
- coaching differential;
- QB concentration / backup penalty;
- depth concentration.

The model should test whether these interactions explain **actual margin minus the market line available at prediction time**.

That asks the right question: what do your grades know that the market has not fully priced yet?

## P1-H — AP-rank quality points are discontinuous and likely fragile

The current quality system has threshold rules for top-5/top-10/top-25 wins and penalties based on ranked/unranked losses. It may have helped in the 2025 development data, but structurally it creates arbitrary cliffs.

Examples:

- beating #25 and beating #26 can receive materially different credit;
- a narrow loss to #26 can be treated very differently from a blowout loss to #25;
- ordinal poll rank is not a continuous measure of team strength;
- rankings themselves incorporate public expectations and results already reflected elsewhere.

Do **not** delete the current term immediately. Register and test a challenger that replaces it with a continuous result signal, e.g.:

`performance_residual = actual_margin - pregame_expected_margin`

Then exponentially decay/partially pool that residual and opponent-adjust it. This uses *how a team performed relative to expectation*, not whether an opponent happened to sit on one side of a poll cutoff.

## P1-I — Production is not currently using the richer public-data engines as a true ensemble

The production config uses the grade rater. `engine.Model` has PPA/Elo/blend support, but `predict.generate()` constructs the model with grades and not the richer stats payload used by a PPA rater. The public-data models also previously failed on their own.

That does **not** mean throw them away. Use them as:

- baselines;
- priors;
- uncertainty features;
- challenger components;
- matchup-context inputs.

A weak standalone model can still add information when combined with a unique private signal, but the blend must prove incremental value out of sample.

## P1-J — The margin-to-win-probability conversion is too rigid for staking

`predict.py` converts predicted margin to win probability with a fixed logistic scale (e.g. CFB sigma constant). That may be acceptable for a display estimate, but not sufficient for economic EV/Kelly sizing.

Probability calibration should be learned prospectively from model residuals and evaluated with:

- Brier score;
- log loss;
- calibration intercept/slope;
- reliability diagram / expected calibration error;
- sharpness/resolution;
- comparison with de-vigged market probability.

Research in probabilistic forecasting and a 2024 sports-betting paper both support prioritizing calibration over raw classification accuracy when probabilities drive wagers.

Until probability calibration is demonstrated on prospective data, keep Kelly out of the official strategy. Flat small research units are safer and statistically cleaner.

## P1-K — Moneyline “EV” is not yet validated

The repository itself already found that historically the largest claimed moneyline EVs performed worst and that the old positive-EV moneyline board was negative overall. The edge-realization shrinkage is a sensible defensive patch, but it is fitted from the same limited grade season.

Treat ML as a shadow experiment until a prospective calibrated probability model beats the market baseline.

## P1-L — Totals need their own model and eligibility threshold

The current system can emit over/under direction for any nonzero disagreement. Totals are a different prediction problem from spreads.

A better structure is:

`Expected Total = Expected Possessions × (Home expected points/possession + Away expected points/possession)`

Inputs should include pace, offensive/defensive efficiency, explosiveness, finishing drives, run/pass tendency, weather/wind, quarterback availability, OL/DL effects, garbage-time behavior, and game-state style.

Then calibrate uncertainty and require a minimum edge that is learned from held-out/prospective results.

# 5. P1 data opportunities that are available now

CollegeFootballData's current API exposes more than the production model is using, including:

- historical betting lines;
- play-by-play with PPA;
- advanced game/season statistics;
- havoc;
- player usage;
- player season PPA;
- returning production;
- transfer portal entries;
- roster data;
- 247Sports Team Talent Composite;
- recruiting ratings aggregated by position group;
- team ratings including CORE, SP+, SRS, Elo, and FPI where available;
- game weather;
- venue location/elevation/timezone data.

Important warning: CFBD states its historical CORE ratings are retrospective results of the released methodology, not a record of exactly what the live model would have published at that historical point. Do not create a leakage-heavy backtest by treating retrospective rating snapshots as contemporaneous forecasts.

For features that must be historically honest, derive them from prior games/rosters or preserve snapshots prospectively.

# 6. Availability/injury automation is now much more feasible

The existing `roster_watch.py` is creative: it maps an unavailable player's removal into the same grade scale. However, ESPN injury data are incomplete for college football and EA-derived depth weights are not the same as real snap/usage value.

For 2026, conference reporting improves this significantly:

- **Big Ten:** official reports three days, two days, one day, and two hours before conference games, with standardized designations.
- **SEC:** standardized public football availability reporting for conference games, with pregame status categories and public conference reports.
- **ACC:** also maintains a current football availability reporting page.

Build conference-specific adapters and treat official reports as higher-confidence sources than generic roster flags.

A player impact model should combine:

`position importance × player usage × player quality above replacement × backup quality × matchup sensitivity × status probability`

Example status priors can initially map Probable/Questionable/Doubtful/Out to rough play probabilities, but those mappings must be calibrated from observed conference-report outcomes rather than assumed forever.

# 7. Home field is plausible, but should become contextual

A fixed `hfa = 4.0` is not obviously absurd: a 2024 collegiate study using 12 seasons estimated an overall home-field effect around 4.1 points at median covariates and found mechanisms involving crowd density, stadium familiarity, travel, time zones, and weather.

That research is an argument **against** treating HFA as one eternal constant. V2 can test a contextual home-field component using:

- neutral site;
- venue capacity/crowd proxy;
- travel distance;
- time-zone crossings;
- altitude/elevation;
- team familiarity / conference matchup;
- dome/weather;
- current-era shrinkage.

Keep the fixed 4.0 Champion value until a challenger proves better prospectively.

# 8. Statistical evaluation changes

## Keep

- MAE
- RMSE
- ATS percentage
- Wilson interval
- ROI
- calibration slope for margin

## Add

### Forecast metrics

- market MAE/RMSE on the exact same games;
- model-minus-market delta MAE/RMSE;
- paired bootstrap CI for model-vs-market error difference;
- residual bias by favorite size, week, conference, home/away, and season phase;
- CRPS if a full margin distribution is produced.

### Probability metrics

- Brier score;
- log loss;
- calibration intercept and slope;
- reliability bins;
- ECE as a descriptive supplement;
- market de-vigged probability benchmark.

### Strategy metrics

- W-L-P at locked line;
- actual ROI at locked price;
- line CLV;
- price CLV;
- percentage of signals beating close;
- average edge at lock;
- max drawdown;
- number of signals;
- performance by model version.

### Research integrity

- number of experiments attempted;
- model promotions/rejections;
- primary metric defined before result;
- feature availability timestamp audit;
- percentage of forecasts with complete provenance.

# 9. What I would trust today

## High trust

- that the codebase is making a serious effort to preserve chronology and expose failure;
- that the hand grades contain information worth studying;
- that the current model can generate reproducible-looking football forecasts from its inputs;
- that several previously dangerous operational bugs have been identified and repaired rather than hidden.

## Medium trust

- current predicted margins as a *research signal*;
- the notion that the grade model may have some ATS value inside its supported range;
- current scale calibration as a reasonable starting point for 2026.

## Low trust / not yet proven

- a claim that the system has a durable profitable edge;
- Kelly stake sizing;
- large moneyline EV values;
- a lifetime ATS/ROI headline that does not distinguish locked line from close and forecast universe from strategy signals;
- any 2025 result presented as untouched confirmation after the amount of iterative development already performed on 2025;
- a raw edge size as confidence;
- any “CLV” metric that is actually model disagreement.

# 10. Immediate implementation order

1. **Correct the record definitions:** locked-line outcome vs closing-line outcome.
2. **Separate forecasts, signals, and actual bets.**
3. **Add spread/total prices and provider-specific market quotes.**
4. **Add immutable model/data provenance to every forecast.**
5. **Move unique state out of Actions cache into a durable event journal/store.**
6. **Freeze the Champion and start prospective shadow challengers.**
7. **Standardize T-72/T-24/T-6/T-2/close snapshots.**
8. **Build matchup-interaction residual model using the hand grades.**
9. **Build official availability adapters (Big Ten/SEC/ACC first).**
10. **Replace AP-threshold quality points only after a continuous challenger wins.**
11. **Build a separate totals model.**
12. **Add probability calibration and only then revisit economic EV/Kelly.**
